1. Overview of Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration is the metabolic pathway that breaks down glucose and produces ATP. The stages of cellular respiration include glycolysis, pyruvate oxidation, the citric acid (Krebs) cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation.

  • Glycolysis: Occurs in the cytosol; splits 1 glucose (6C) into 2 pyruvate (3C) molecules. Net gain: 2 ATP and 2 NADH.
  • Pyruvate Oxidation: Occurs in the mitochondrial matrix; converts pyruvate into Acetyl-CoA, releasing CO₂ and generating NADH.
  • Citric Acid Cycle: Completes breakdown of glucose derivatives into CO₂, producing 2 ATP, 6 NADH, and 2 FADH₂ per glucose.
  • Oxidative Phosphorylation: Electron transport chain (ETC) and chemiosmosis across the inner mitochondrial membrane yield ~26–28 ATP.
